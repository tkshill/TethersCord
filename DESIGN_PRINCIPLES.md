# Design Principles

The ten core principles behind TethersCord. `ROADMAP.md` is forward-looking and
`CHANGELOG.md` records what shipped; this document is the standard every rule,
mechanic, and interface decision is measured against.

## 1. The goal is to have fun

Not to make a simulation, or do a thought experiment, or experience a power
fantasy, or perform a creative exercise. The joy of the People at the table is
the final metric of every rule, moment, and interaction.

## 2. If it matters, it should be part of the game's mechanics

Reward what you want to see in the game. But you cannot cover every lane, so be
judicious in deciding what absolutely must exist to manifest the game you
envision. The game mechanics should turn interest and effort into rewards and
enjoyable moments at the table. Playing the game should help one have success in
the game. Success should enable more interesting play.

## 3. The Table tells the story

The Facilitator's part is to provide scaffolding and support to the other
players, reward interest, and express context. The Character Players' jobs are to
propel the events of their characters' lives, take actions, and deepen the world
through their decisions and ideas. Everyone is responsible for enabling the
success and creativity of each other. The game belongs to the entire table.

## 4. The game is sustained by the shared fiction

What the table agrees is true, is true. What the table agrees is relevant is
relevant. This is not to enable the browbeating of the individual by the
majority, but to establish that the game's rules cannot, and do not seek to,
explicitly define every moment and interaction. So the final arbiter is simply
whether everyone can be on the same page, in a way that we all continue to enjoy
the game. The goal is to empower the individual to be creative, take risks, and
express discomfort, knowing that doing so is itself an expression of the game's
rules and principles. The table's desires never take precedence over the
suffering of a Player. A Player's desires never take precedence over the
suffering of the Table.

## 5. Characters should grow in depth, not strength

Power loss should accompany power gain. Excitement is in the potential to
overcome one's challenges, not the certainty. Characters should be dynamic and
revealed through play and action. Players must be rewarded for effort even when
their characters are punished for failure. Protagonists have stakes. They must
have desires and conflicts that matter beyond the loss of their own lives and the
capacity to change the course of their own destiny.

## 6. Avoid math and magic numbers

Whenever possible, prefer names with meaning. This is not a calculation game
(nothing wrong with calculation games, but this isn't that). Don't use two things
where one would suffice. Make it easy to go from intent to outcome. Make every
decision matter and avoid outcomes that negate Player choices. Every additional
game element must have a reward greater than its cognitive burden.

## 7. It should still be a game of strategy, chance, and asymmetry

This game uses a degree of dice-roll randomness to tap into the excitement of
uncertainty, loss of control, and spontaneous narrative beats. It uses resource
management to reward creativity, attentiveness, and problem solving. It is
designed with a Facilitator not as a leader, commander, or creator, but because
having asymmetrical roles can relieve the pressure on all Players to feel like
they must equally express all parts of play. The Character Players guide their
characters through the obstacles of having Wants and Desires in a finite world;
the Facilitator attempts to present a world that enables the efforts, successes,
and tragedies of the Character Players.

## 8. Players are not Protagonists

The aim is for Players to have a good time as much as possible during play.
Protagonists, however, should frequently be in states of conflict, turmoil, and
struggle. By design, Players must voluntarily put their characters in situations
of weakness, uncertainty, fragility, and vulnerability. Characters must fail to
succeed, and the more Players choose to fail the more opportunity there is to
influence success. There is no accidental end in this game. We're aiming for a
game where characters must live with consequences.

## 9. Avoid minutiae

Some games have lots of details. The details are the fun. This is not that game.
Anything written should matter in BIG, bold, interesting ways. When in doubt, ask
what the shared fiction implies. Ask what would make sense AND be interesting.
Prefer one word that the Table has shared context on versus several words for
pre-emptive precision. The reality that everyone paints a slightly different
mental image during play is a feature, not a bug.

## 10. Remove mechanics until anything less would compromise the principles above

Less and less until your players and play-testers ask for more. And even then,
try to identify what feeling, emotion, or experience they actually desire, and
then decide whether achieving that is best achieved through a new rule or
mechanic. Do you need more, or do you just need better? Better clarity, better
accuracy, better order, better dialogue. Aim for a few things done well instead
of many things to shore up many frail things.
