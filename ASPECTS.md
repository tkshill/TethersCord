# Aspects — a reference for character creation

Every character in TethersCord is written around three **aspects**: an
**Archetype**, a **Desire**, and a **Quest**. This document is a bank of examples
to pull from, or push against, when you write yours. `DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md` holds
the reasoning; `CLAUDE.md` covers how aspects behave in play.

A curated handful of these also show up in the app, under "see examples" on each
aspect field.

## What an aspect is

An aspect is a short, plain sentence that is **always true** about your
character. It is never a rating and never a skill — the game has no numbers on
the sheet. In a roll, spending a boon to **Highlight** an aspect is you saying
"this is true right now, and it should matter," and it tilts the odds your way
for that one roll.

Aspects only ever **accumulate Banes**. A mixed roll drops a Bane onto one of
your three at random, and they stay there — there is currently no mechanic that
clears them. So aspects are written to hold tension from the start — something
about them is already in conflict with the world.

## What makes a strong aspect

- **True now, not aspirational.** "A knight" beats "wants to be a knight" —
  write the second as a Desire instead.
- **In tension with the world.** The table should be able to make it cost you
  something. "Kind to strangers" is thin; "cannot walk past someone worse off"
  has a hook.
- **A stance, a hunger, or a debt** — not a competence. Avoid "good with a
  blade," "a skilled forger." Those are things you just *do* in the fiction.
- **One idea.** If it has an "and" in it, it is probably two aspects.
- **Concrete enough to picture.** A specific image ("the last of a drowned
  village") gives everyone something to pull on; a category ("an outsider")
  does not.
- **Leaves room to be wrong about the character.** The best aspects can be
  turned against the person who wrote them.

## How the three fit together

- **Archetype** — who the world takes you for. The role, the reputation, the
  first thing a stranger assumes. It is about other people's expectations.
- **Desire** — what you want badly enough to risk things for. Private, unmet,
  and strong enough to pull you into danger.
- **Quest** — the concrete thing you are trying to do right now. It changes as
  the fiction moves; the Archetype and Desire under it are slower.

A good set has friction between the three: the Quest strains the Desire, or the
Archetype gets in the way of the Quest.

## Archetype — who the world takes you for

- The soldier who was told the war was over
- Youngest heir of a house that no longer exists
- The healer who is afraid of their own hands
- A translator for a people who have stopped speaking
- The cartographer of a country being erased
- Everyone's second choice, and they know it
- The prophet nobody asked for
- Last apprentice of a dead trade
- The one who was supposed to have died in the fire
- A judge in a place with no more law
- The family disappointment, home again
- The stranger every town assumes it already knows
- The bodyguard of someone who no longer needs one
- A saint to people who have never met them
- The negotiator both sides stopped trusting
- The child of a famous traitor
- Keeper of a shrine to a god that left
- The retired name that still opens doors
- A doctor in a plague their training does not cover
- The only witness anyone believes

## Desire — what you would risk things for

- To be believed, just once, by the person who raised them
- To find the room where the decision about them was made
- To go one season without owing anyone
- To be the one who stays
- To make the thing that outlasts the maker
- To stop performing the version of themselves that works
- To be forgiven without having to ask
- To hear their own name said kindly
- To matter to a place, not just pass through it
- To keep a promise they made to someone who is gone
- To be needed less
- To finish something — anything — for once
- To prove the people who left were wrong to
- To be allowed to rest without it being a defeat
- To hold power long enough to give it away well
- To be known for the work and not the name
- To bring one person home safe and call it even
- To stop being afraid of the same thing every day
- To be chosen first
- To undo the one thing

## Quest — what you are trying to do right now

- Get the last shipment across the border before the pass closes
- Sit with the council long enough to hear them say the name
- Keep the lights on in the house until spring
- Bring back proof that the village is still there
- Deliver the letter without reading it
- Finish the map of the flooded quarter
- Get the child to the coast and onto a boat
- Buy back the debt before it is sold on
- Reach the archive before it is moved again
- Keep the peace talks going for three more days
- Find out who signed the order
- Get the harvest in with half the hands
- Carry the body home for burial
- Talk the garrison into standing down
- Trade the heirloom for passage and not regret it
- Hold the bridge until the others are across
- Track down the last person who remembers the route
- Get the medicine distributed before it is confiscated
- Convince one more family to leave
- Return what was taken, quietly, before anyone notices it is back

## Worked characters

Three aspects together, with the friction between them noted.

- **The soldier who was told the war was over** / *To be believed, just once,
  by the person who raised them* / **Get the last shipment across the border
  before the pass closes.** — The Quest needs him to move like the war is still
  on; the Desire needs him to go home and be still.

- **The healer who is afraid of their own hands** / *To be needed less* /
  **Keep the child alive through the night.** — Everything the Quest asks is the
  thing the Archetype dreads and the Desire is trying to escape.

- **Keeper of a shrine to a god that left** / *To matter to a place, not just
  pass through it* / **Convince one more family to leave the valley before the
  flood.** — Getting the Quest right empties the only place the Desire has ever
  had.

- **The family disappointment, home again** / *To be forgiven without having to
  ask* / **Settle the estate without the house being sold.** — The Quest puts
  her back in every room the Archetype was formed in, in front of the people
  whose forgiveness the Desire wants.

- **A judge in a place with no more law** / *To hold power long enough to give
  it away well* / **Talk the garrison into standing down.** — The Archetype is
  the only reason the garrison might listen, and using it that way is exactly
  the power the Desire is trying not to keep.

## Rewriting a strained aspect

TethersCord used to force a rewrite automatically, through an "untether"
mechanic tied to a failed session goal; that trigger has been retired, and
session goals no longer resolve with a roll at all. The rewrite itself is
still good practice whenever the table feels an aspect's accumulated Banes
have made it stop fitting the fiction — it's just a judgment call now, not an
automatic event. A rewrite changes what the aspect **means** — it never adds a
rating or a power (principle 5: grow in depth, not strength). Some shapes a
rewrite can take:

- **Reword the same idea, harder.** "The soldier who was told the war was over"
  becomes "The soldier who kept fighting after everyone went home."
- **Turn it inside out.** "The healer who is afraid of their own hands" becomes
  "The healer who has stopped flinching, and misses the fear."
- **Name what the strain did.** The Banes were about betrayal; the new
  Archetype is "The one who assumes the worst now, and is usually right."
- **Replace it entirely** with something a scene made true, if the old idea has
  nowhere left to go.
